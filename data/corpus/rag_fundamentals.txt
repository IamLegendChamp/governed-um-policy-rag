AgentForge RAG Overview
-----------------------
RAG means Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The system first searches a knowledge base for relevant passages, then sends those passages to a language model so answers stay grounded in facts.

Naive RAG uses a simple pipeline: chunk documents, embed chunks, retrieve top-k similar chunks for a user question, and paste them into the prompt.

Hybrid RAG combines keyword search (BM25) with dense vector search. Keyword search finds exact terms like product codes; dense search finds paraphrases and concepts.

Advanced RAG adds steps before and after retrieval: query rewriting, reranking, and filtering by metadata such as date or tenant.

Self-RAG and Corrective RAG add checks when retrieved text is low quality. The system may search again or refuse to answer instead of hallucinating.

Production RAG tracks retrieval hit rate, latency, and faithfulness scores. Eval tools like Ragas measure whether answers match the retrieved context.

Chunking strategy matters. Fixed-size chunks are easy but may cut sentences in half. Recursive chunking respects paragraphs and headings. Overlap between chunks preserves context across boundaries.

pgvector and managed vector databases store embeddings for fast similarity search at scale. For learning, local TF-IDF or small embedding models are enough.

When context is too long for the model, trim chunks by relevance score or use a parent-document pattern: retrieve small chunks but return the full parent section to the LLM.
